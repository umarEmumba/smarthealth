<objective>
Finish M1 across the service boundary: completing a visit must complete its appointment. Plan 02.1-01 added `checked_in -> completed` in Scheduling and plan 02.1-06 made check-in move the appointment to `checked_in`; here the visit-completion workflow tells Scheduling the visit is over, so the appointment's state machine reaches its terminal `completed` state and the slot stays reserved for good.

Claim verification (done while planning, reproducible): `VisitCompletedWorkflow` runs billing finalize, a follow-up intent and an analytics intent; nothing contacts Scheduling, so a completed visit leaves its appointment non-terminal. The claim is real.

Design (planner discretion, called out): the appointment step is a FOURTH concurrent branch of the existing gather (not a sequential step after it) so it can never be starved by a slow or retrying billing branch, and it is added behind `workflow.patched` with the long `COMPENSATION_ACTIVITY` retry budget (the visit is already committed; Scheduling must catch up). Each step that must land also writes a settlement marker in `visit_db` (a timestamp and an outcome string): `billing_settled_at`/`billing_outcome` and `appointment_settled_at`/`appointment_outcome`. A business refusal (`not_checked_in`, for visits that were checked in before this phase and whose appointment is still `confirmed`; `no_appointment`) is a terminal outcome and also writes its marker, so nothing loops forever. Plan 02.1-10 builds the billing retry and the reconciler on these markers. Pre-existing visits are synthetic dev data; they are not backfilled and settle as `not_checked_in`.

Output: migration `visit_0002`, marker repository functions, the Scheduling reference call, the patched fourth branch, the new activity, and proofs including a recorded history.
</objective>

<objective>
Close mentor item M1 (Week 2 must-fix 1) inside Scheduling: add `checked_in`, `completed` and `no_show` appointment states, a DB-clock check-in window, and the refusal of cancel/reschedule once the patient has checked in or the visit is over. This plan leads the phase with a tracer: one confirmed appointment is moved to `checked_in` through the real internal route with a Visit service token, and a cancel attempt on it is then refused, proven on live Postgres.

Claim verification (done while planning, reproducible): `AppointmentStatus` has only pending/confirmed/cancelled/failed; Visit's check-in (`services/visit/api/visits.py`) never writes to Scheduling, so a checked-in or even completed visit leaves its appointment `confirmed`; `appointment_repo.cancel_appointment` and `swap_slot` accept any `confirmed` appointment, so cancel/reschedule free the slot and the cancel workflow issues a refund. The claim is real.

Purpose: the appointment state machine is a graded Week 2 deliverable and the root of two corruption paths (rebooked slot, wrongful refund).

Output: migration `scheduling_0004`, widened constraints, state constants, four repository transitions, two internal routes, refusal messages, and a proof suite. Visit's use of these routes lands in plan 02.1-06 (check-in) and 02.1-09 (completion); the automatic no-show sweep lands in plan 02.1-11.

Decisions honoured: M1 (CONTEXT). Planner discretion (called out): check-in window default opens 60 minutes before the slot and closes at the slot's end; no-show grace default 30 minutes after the slot ends; `no_show` is reached only by the sweep in plan 02.1-11 (no manual staff endpoint, not requested by the review); the new internal routes accept service tokens only (staff reach check-in through Visit's public route, which owns the visit record).
